Phone Policy Friction Audit

Your policy, or the ISBE template, run against five channels. Illinois Public Act 104-0657 regulates the first, exempts the second, and does not mention the other three. A compliant policy is a record that a decision was made. This audit asks what the record leaves open.

The text we are auditing
Which clock we are on

A district whose policy before July 28, 2026 already limited devices for most or all of the school day may keep it through 2030–31.

No policy in hand? Audit this practice excerpt

During instruction, personal phones stay in classroom storage. Class tasks move to district laptops. Students may use an enabled AI feature to draft a response before reading the sources. The school reviews phone referrals each month and describes fewer referrals as evidence that learning has improved. An approved support may be retrieved through the office, but the procedure names no backup if the office route is unavailable.

A fictional excerpt for practice. Not a model policy. It touches all five channels and settles none of them well.

Five channels, three questions

The core of the audit
What does our policy actually say?

Quote it if you can. “Nothing” is a legitimate and common answer, and it is a finding.

What is the gap?

The distance between what the text says and what happens in a classroom on an ordinary Tuesday.

Who owns it, and by when?

A name, not a committee, and a date. A gap with no owner is the most common result.

Five implementation questions

What the template leaves to you

The ISBE template settles the definition, the required exceptions, and the limits on enforcement. It does not settle how any of it runs on a school day. Mark each question decided only when you could describe the answer to a parent.

Access risk register

Before conditions change

A quiet classroom can still exclude a learner. Name each risk as a specific student situation rather than a group label, then give it an owner and a date to check it.

Stakeholder conversations

Section 10-20.88(f)

The statute requires input from the teachers’ bargaining agent (if the district has one), administrators, and parents or guardians when the policy is written and at each review, and it encourages student input. Two conversations the work depends on are not named at all.

Evidence plan

Keep three questions apart

Write what you will actually collect, not what you would like to know. Then sort each measure by the question it answers. Is the restriction workable? is implementation. Can students participate? is access. What can students explain or do? is learning.

No learning measure yet. Confiscation counts, enforcement time, and exception requests show whether the policy is running. None of them shows whether students can explain or do more than before.

For the adopting vote

    The claim to avoid

    This audit will not tell you whether a phone policy worked, and nothing will for at least a year. Falling confiscation counts answer whether the restriction is workable. They say nothing about what students can explain or do. Make claims no stronger than the evidence permits; you will be quoted on them.
